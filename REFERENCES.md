## 2. SMR benchmarking and its limitations (10)

Used in: Introduction ¶1, Discussion ¶1 and ¶3.

12. **Rating the quality of intensive care units: is it a function of the ICU scoring
    system?** *Crit Care Med* 2002. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/12352029/
    — *different scores give different quality verdicts; direct precedent for our design*
13. **Kramer AA, Higgins TL, Zimmerman JE. Comparing observed and predicted mortality
    among ICUs using different prognostic systems: why do performance assessments
    differ?** *Crit Care Med.* 2015;43(2):261–269.
    doi:10.1097/CCM.0000000000000694 — https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/25365725/
    **[FULL CITATION VERIFIED]**
    — 89,353 admissions, 47 units, 36 US hospitals, 2008–2013. APACHE IVa vs an
    NQF-endorsed model **agreed on significance and direction of SMR only 45% of the
    time**; NQF excluded 27.9% of patients vs 10.6% for APACHE IVa.
    **Key comparator for our Discussion:** prior work shows that changing the entire
    model flips >half of performance verdicts. We show that a single already-documented
    variable moves 23.5% of quartiles and 8% of outlier flags — a far cheaper
    intervention for a comparable effect. Note Zimmerman is the APACHE IV developer,
    which strengthens the citation considerably.
14. **The effect of ICU admission patterns on mortality-based critical care performance
    measures.** *Ann Am Thorac Soc*. https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5018925/
    — *closest methodological analogue; also our target journal*
15. **Preventable mortality evaluation in the ICU.** *Crit Care*.
    https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3681346/ — *“statistically unexpected death
    is not preventable death”*
16. **Evaluating risk-adjusted hospital performance using large-scale ICU mortality data:
    a flexible semi-nonparametric approach.**
    https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10085625/
17. **Risk-adjusting mortality in the nationwide Veterans Affairs healthcare system.**
    https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC9640507/
18. **Misspecification issues in risk adjustment and construction of outcome-based
    quality indicators.** *Health Serv Outcomes Res Methodol*.
    https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10742-006-0014-z
19. **Improving hospital quality risk-adjustment models using interactions identified by
    hierarchical group lasso regularisation.** *BMC Health Serv Res* 2023.
    https://bmchealthservres.biomedcentral.com/articles/10.1186/s12913-023-10423-9
20. **Risk adjustment in quality measurement.** CMS Measures Management System.
    https://mmshub.cms.gov/sites/default/files/Risk-Adjustment-in-Quality-Measurement.pdf
21. **Nerenz DR, Austin JM, Deutscher D, Joynt Maddox KE, Nuccio EJ, Teigland C,
    Weinhandl E, Glance LG. Adjusting Quality Measures For Social Risk Factors Can
    Promote Equity In Health Care.** *Health Aff (Millwood).* 2021;40(4):637–644.
    doi:10.1377/hlthaff.2020.01764 **[FULL CITATION VERIFIED. PDF in refs.]**
    — **the central methodological analogy.** Adjust and you may mask real differences.
    Do not adjust and you penalise institutions serving different populations. Frames
